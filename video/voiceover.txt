[HOOK 0:00-0:15]
Under the counter of almost every spaza shop in South Africa, there is a
school exercise book. The ibhuku. Who owes what, in pencil. Stock counted
by eye. Expiry dates nobody has time to check.

[MEET SISI 0:15-0:35]
Meet Sisi. A WhatsApp assistant for spaza owners. No app to install,
nothing new to learn. She speaks English and isiZulu, by text or voice
note. And she never makes a mess of your records.

[DEMO 0:35-2:00]
MaZulu asks: how much does Sipho owe? Sisi checks the credit book.
Seventy-three rand. Every cent accounted for.

A sale is one message. Stock drops by itself. And when the shelf runs
low, Sisi says so before the customer does.

No typing needed. A voice note in her own words becomes a neat book
entry — always confirmed first.

A photo of the fridge, and Sisi reads every date stamp. Expired milk,
off the shelf, before it can harm anyone.

At seven, one word: cash up. Cash, book sales and payments in,
totalled in seconds.

And the paperwork: permits, the spaza fund, and a warning that
applications are free — because scammers charge for what costs nothing.

[HOW IT WORKS 2:00-2:30]
Behind Sisi: Meta's Muse Spark reads and reasons. Muse Voice Transcribe
hears every voice note. And vision checks every shelf, with dates in
strict structured output. Every number is confirmed by the owner — and
undone with one word. Your records stay yours. Say delete my data, and
they're gone.

[IMPACT 2:30-2:52]
One hundred and fifty thousand spaza shops feed South Africa's
townships. Five percent of GDP. Millions of jobs. Sisi gives each owner
a sister who never forgets. Built with Meta's models. Meet Sisi.
